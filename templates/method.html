Composite Simpson's 1/3 Rule

For an integral \( \int_a^b f(x) \, dx \), we split \([a,b]\) into \(n\) subintervals where \(n\) is even. Each pair of subintervals is approximated by a quadratic polynomial (Simpson's rule).

Formula

\[ \int_a^b f(x) \, dx \approx \frac{h}{3} \left[ f(x_0) + 4\sum_{i=1,3,5,\dots}^{n-1} f(x_i) + 2\sum_{i=2,4,6,\dots}^{n-2} f(x_i) + f(x_n) \right] \] where
  • \( h = \frac{b-a}{n} \)
  • Alternating coefficients: 4 (odd), 2 (even), starting from \(x_1\).

Error Bound

If \( f^{(4)} \) is continuous on \([a,b]\), the error is:

\[ E = -\frac{(b-a)^5}{180n^4} f^{(4)}(c), \quad c \in [a,b] \]

When to use it?

  • The function is smooth (continuous fourth derivative).
  • You need higher accuracy than the trapezoidal rule.
  • \(n\) must be even – the calculator checks that.
